# DesignProAI OS — Enterprise & Channel Use Cases

This document is for enterprise buyers and potential distribution/channel partners. It explains where DesignProAI fits commercially without changing the core product definition.

## Franchise / multi-location network

### Problem

Each location can end up with a different design process, different file practices, different proofing habits, and different production preparation standards.

### DesignProAI role

DesignProAI provides a common design-to-production operating layer:

- shared app ecosystem;
- consistent prompt-to-design workflow;
- common RevisionStudio proof/revision process;
- common production-file ordering path;
- common GENIE geometry/panelizer workflow;
- common QC and output-delivery model.

### Business value

Standardize capability without requiring every location to build the same software/process stack independently.

## Printer / equipment manufacturer

### Problem

The manufacturer supplies hardware that produces the final graphics, but the customer's design-to-production workflow upstream of the printer remains fragmented.

### DesignProAI role

Provide the software layer that helps move work from customer brief to validated production files before it reaches the RIP/device.

### Commercial model

Potential enterprise/OEM bundle, dealer-channel offer, or recurring software-revenue partnership.

### Positioning

**Hardware produces the job. DesignProAI helps create and prepare the job.**

---

## Film / media manufacturer or distributor

### Problem

The supplier already has recurring relationships with shops buying film/media, but the relationship is primarily consumables-based.

### DesignProAI role

Offer a daily-use software product to the same customer base.

### Commercial model

Qualified channel/reseller program with recurring subscription participation where contractually approved.

### Strategic benefit

A digital recurring-revenue layer sits alongside recurring material sales.

---

## Industry educator / training organization

### Problem

A class or certification creates a high-trust relationship but often ends after the event.

### DesignProAI role

Give students/shops an operating platform they can continue using after training.

### Commercial model

Partner/referral/reseller recurring revenue, co-branded onboarding, training-specific packages, or enterprise cohorts.

### Strategic benefit

Education becomes an ongoing operating relationship rather than a one-time event.

---

## Industry representative / existing sales network

### Problem

Experienced reps already know the market and have trusted relationships, but adding new physical product lines may not create meaningful recurring economics.

### DesignProAI role

Give the rep a software product that can be sold into existing accounts.

### Commercial model

Qualified account attribution + recurring subscription percentage, subject to partner agreement.

### Strategic benefit

One closed software account can produce recurring revenue while the customer remains subscribed.

---

## Large fleet / enterprise graphics operation

### Problem

Fleet graphics live beyond one print run. Vehicles are added, panels are damaged, logos change, locations change, and new vehicle models enter the fleet.

### DesignProAI role

Persistent project lineage:

- GenerationID;
- DesignID;
- Revision history;
- approved proofs;
- production file relationship;
- downstream replacement/reorder context.

### Business value

Reduce file archaeology and make future changes/reorders part of the same managed design asset rather than a new search/rebuild exercise.

---

## Production provider / print partner

### Problem

Incoming files vary widely in quality, lineage, and readiness.

### DesignProAI role

Move work through a standardized production path with GENIE geometry, PrintPanelStudio QC, output verification, and WrapBox delivery before downstream printing.

### Business value

A clearer production handoff and a more consistent file-quality process.

# Partner-program structure to define separately

The software docs should explain the product. The commercial agreement should define partner economics.

A partner agreement needs explicit rules for:

- referral vs. authorized reseller vs. OEM/strategic partner;
- percentage/commission basis;
- recurring-revenue duration;
- first-six-month or launch incentives;
- account ownership;
- enterprise deal registration;
- downline/secondary rep attribution if offered;
- refunds/chargebacks;
- support responsibilities;
- branding/co-marketing;
- termination and treatment of existing MRR.

Do not hard-code commission percentages into the platform documentation. Keep commercial terms in the partner program/order form so they can evolve without rewriting technical/product docs.
